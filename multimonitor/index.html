Getting started

1. Turn on the camera's Wi-Fi connection to a smartphone (on the camera: Wi-Fi → connection to a smartphone).
2. In the app tap Add camera. The app looks for the camera by itself: on your phone's personal hotspot, on the camera's own Wi-Fi or on a router you both use.
3. Tap the camera it finds. The first time, iOS asks for access to the local network — allow it, the app talks to the cameras over it.
4. Some cameras ask you to confirm the connection on their screen.

A camera takes one controller at a time. Close LUMIX Sync (and any other remote app) before you connect.

The camera list

Every saved camera has a status: offline, connecting and connected. The link icon connects a camera — even when it moved to another network, the app finds it again. The camera icon opens its picture; the crossed link disconnects it.

Edit renames cameras, removes them and changes their order — the order of the tiles in the grid. Saved cameras can connect on their own when the app starts.

The grid and REC ALL

All connected cameras side by side. The red button in the middle starts recording on every camera. To stop them all, hold it — a single tap never stops a take. A camera that records shows REC and the time on its tile; a red frame marks it.

The app never stops a recording by itself: when the link drops, the phone locks or the app goes to the background, the cameras keep recording.

The bar under the grid holds the overlays and four Fn buttons you choose yourself. Display sets what the tiles show: camera names, frame rate and picture age, function buttons.

Picture overlays and LUTs

Focus peaking marks the sharp edges, false color paints exposure levels, zebra stripes the bright areas. Each can be on for all cameras or for some of them (the small 1/4 tag tells how many); colours and thresholds are in the camera settings.

Each camera can show a LUT (.cube) applied before the overlays — one is built in, and you can import your own from the Files app.

The overlays read the camera's live view: a compressed preview of what the camera shows. Use them to judge focus and exposure, not as a measuring instrument.

One camera: focus, exposure, zoom

Tap a tile to open the camera full screen. Tap the picture to move the focus point there. The pill at the bottom shows aperture, shutter and ISO — tap a value and turn the dial to change it. Zoom enlarges the picture 1.5–4× to check focus.

Settings

Language (English, Polish), theme (Dark, or Sun for bright daylight), your licence and restore purchases, help and a diagnostic log you can share when something goes wrong. The tips above the camera list can be hidden and brought back in Settings › Help.

Free and Pro

One camera is free with every feature. Pro is a one-time purchase that unlocks up to four cameras at once — no subscription. Bought on another device? Restore purchases in Settings.

Cameras

Panasonic LUMIX cameras with Wi-Fi remote control. Tested on the LUMIX S5II / S5IIX; other recent LUMIX bodies with the same remote protocol are expected to work.

Troubleshooting

  • The camera is not found — check that its Wi-Fi connection to a smartphone is on and that LUMIX Sync is closed. On an iPhone hotspot, try turning Maximize Compatibility on if the camera cannot see the hotspot.
  • Nothing connects — allow Local Network access: iOS Settings › Privacy & Security › Local Network.
  • REC fails with “busy” — the camera is in a menu or in playback; close it on the camera.
  • Still stuck? Share the diagnostic log from Settings › Help and contact us.
